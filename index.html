Why we exist

It started with one blackboard, borrowed from a wedding pandal.

In the summer of 2019, five of us began teaching eleven children under a neem tree in Mandvi because the nearest school bus had stopped coming. We had no plan, no funding and no name — just a blackboard, a box of chalk, and the stubborn belief that showing up consistently matters more than showing up impressively.

Today, GNU Seva Foundation runs learning centres, tailoring workshops and health camps across 72 villages. We are deliberately small, deliberately local, and deliberately slow: every program is piloted in one village, measured honestly, and only then scaled. When something fails — and some things have — we write about that too in our annual report.

We are not saving anyone. The villages we work in were never empty of talent; they were empty of opportunity. We just help carry the desks.

Show upConsistency over spectacle. Same faces, same Saturdays, year after year.
Ask firstEvery program begins with a village meeting, not a proposal.

What we do

Four programs, one promise: we stay until it works.

Nothing here is a one-day drive. Each program runs on a multi-year cycle with baseline surveys, village committees and honest exit reviews.

Education

Pathshala Learning Centres

After-school centres in 34 villages with trained local tutors, library corners and weekend math circles. We bridge the gap between the school's bench and the home's kitchen table — where homework actually gets done, or doesn't.

2,350 children enrolled34 centres
Livelihoods

Silai Shakti Tailoring Schools

Six-month tailoring and micro-enterprise courses for women, with machine ownership on completion, market linkages and a self-help-group savings circle. 640 graduates so far; 71% now earn from home.

640 women trainedBatch 13 running
Health

Swasthya Abhiyan Camps

Monthly health camps with volunteer doctors: BP and sugar screening, antenatal check-ups, and a referral fund for families who need the district hospital but fear the bus fare more than the illness.

96 camps held18 volunteer doctors
Community

Udaan Youth Volunteer Corps

College students from Vadodara adopt a village for a year — tutoring, digitising land records for elders, teaching grandparents to video-call their migrant children. Small things. Enormous things.

380 active volunteers9 colleges
